ABSTRACT
The standing wave of the whole angle mode hemispherical resonator gyroscope is in a free precession state. To
achievehighaccuracyandawidemeasurementrange,itisnecessarytocompensateforthedriveerrortoreduce
the control force output error and standing wave drift. In this paper, we propose an error parameter identifi-
cation and compensation method for the drive error, including gain mismatch, electrode angle alignment error,
andX/Y channel phase delay. Firstly, we fully elucidate the mechanism of drive error caused by manufacturing
andX/Ycircuitchannelparametermismatch.Secondly,thedriveerrorismodeledanditsimpactonthestanding
waveazimuthandeachcontrolloopisanalyzed.Thirdly,wepresentahigh-precisionerroridentificationmethod
based on the error model that is not affected by the inherent error of the resonator, such as damping anisotropy
drift. Finally, a series of experiments are carried out to verify the accuracy of the proposed method for error
parameter identification and the effectiveness of the method for HRG performance enhancement. The bias
instabilityofthetestedHRGdecreasesby4timesfrom0.0277◦/hto0.0071◦/h,andthescalefactornonlinearity
decreases by 5 times from 3.92ppm to 0.72ppm.
1. Introduction
Compared with the mechanical gyroscope and optical gyroscope,
hemispherical resonator gyroscope (HRG) has the characteristics of
simple structure, ultra-high accuracy, miniaturization, and no need for
post-maintenanceonceinuse[1–5].Meanwhile,thewholeanglemode
HRG has become the main focus of research nowadays due to its
advantage of a wide dynamic measurement range [6,7]. The whole
angle mode HRG error sources are mainly the inherent drift caused by
the circumferential inhomogeneity of damping and stiffness in the ma-
terial properties of the resonator [8–11]. As well as non-inherent drift
due to the detection and drive system error caused by the mismatch of
the X/Y channel circuit parameters and the limitations of the flat elec-
trode manufacturing and assembly technology.
The HRG detection system as the feedback of the gyroscope closed-
loop control loop, the comprehensive modeling, accurate identifica-
tion,andcompensationofitserrorhavebeenofgreatconcern[12].The
drivesystemactsasaforwardchannelofthecontrolloop.Theeffectof
itserrorontheamplitudeloop,quadratureloop,andphase-lockedloop
canbesuppressedtosomeextentbytheclosed-loopcharacteristics[13].
However, unlike the force to rebalance (FTR) mode, the standing wave
azimuth is in open loop control in the whole angle mode [14,15]. Any
erroneousprojectionofthecontrolforceintherateloopthatisinphase
withthemainwaveofvibrationwillresultindriftinthestandingwave.
In [16], a method for the identification of gain errors as well as
electrodemisalignmentangleisgiven.Theidentificationmethodneeds
the drift caused by the damping of the standing wave at special
azimuthal angles as known. However, under the influence of multiple
drive errors, the quadrature control force will have component in the
rate loop which causes the standing wave drift. Therefore, this method
only has an accurate identification effect for the HRG with a small
quadrature control force, i.e., a small frequency split. The method also
lacks the modeling and compensation for the phase error of the X/Y
drive channels. In [17], the drive electrode misalignment angle is
modeled,andusetheautomaticgaincontrolbyadjustingtheparametric
pump voltage to fix the drive voltage. It should be noted that only the
angle error is considered, and that the gain mismatch and phase error
havenotbeenmodeledorcompensated.In[18]and[19],theformation
mechanism of the electrode misalignment angle and its effect on the
detectionsystemandthedrivesystemintheFTRmodeareelaboratedin

themonlyconsiderspartialdriveerrorsandlacksoverallmodelingand

enhancetheHRGperformancewhentheanalyzederrortermisthemain
error. If other errors are not modeled, on the one hand, the drive error
willnotbefullycompensatedandcontinuetoaffecttheHRGaccuracyin
the drive system. On the other hand, the unmodeled error will be
mistakenlyidentifiedasoneoftheerrortermsconsidered,andtheHRG
may obtain a satisfactory performance under the specific conditions of
compensation experiments. The consistency of performance cannot be
guaranteedwhentheHRGisinactualworkingconditionwithdynamic
speedinputsorwhenotheroptimizationalgorithmstoenhancetheHRG
performance are added, such as damping or nonlinear compensation.
Therefore,onlybyseparatingthedriveerroritembyitemandmodeling
comprehensively can we accurately identify the error parameters,
compensate for the effects of the errors, improve the accuracy of the
whole angle mode HRG, and satisfy the needs of the HRG in high-
precision navigation.
In this paper, we address the problem of modeling, identifying, and
compensatingtheerrorofthewholeanglemodeHRGdrivesystem.The
formation mechanism of each error is introduced. The control force
erroractsonthestandingprimaryandquadraturewavesandtheeffect
on the HRG drift is derived. A drive error identification and compen-
sation method that avoids the effect of resonator damping drift is pro-
posed. Based on actual HRG experiments, the validity and reliability of
the proposed method are verified.
2. Whole angle mode HRG detection and drive
2.1. HRG detection principle
ThestructureoftheHRGisshowninFig.1.Itismainlycomposedof
thehemisphericalresonatorandflatplateelectrode.Thediameterofthe
harmonic oscillator ranges from 20mm to 30mm, which can simulta-
neously meet the requirements for detectable capacitance and minia-
turization. The support rod, the inner surface, and the lip edge of the
hemisphericalresonatorarecoatedwithametalfilmwhichisconnected
asaconductor.Theflatelectrodesarealsocoatedwithametalfilmlayer
andareuniformlyetchedintoeightmutuallyisolatedelectrodesectors.
They form eight capacitors with the resonator to detect and drive
standing wave. When assembling the resonator with the flat plate
electrode, the assembly gap is typically between 20 μm and 70 μm.
The HRG standing wave vibration detection is performed by
applying a DC high voltage source at the resonator support rod. As the
resonator vibrates, the change in detection capacitance reflects the vi-
bration information. Vibration control involves applying a voltage
signal of the same frequency and phase as the resonator to the corre-
sponding flat electrodes. The electrostatic control force is generated
throughcapacitancetomaintaintheresonatorstandingwavetoachieve
thedesiredvibrationstate.ThewholeanglemodeHRGcontroldiagram

The residual fluctuation is mainly due to the following reasons.
Firstly, the control and compensation scheme is realized with digital
circuits. The number of registers and DAC bits limits the quantization
accuracyofthecompensation.Theactualcompensationparametersare
slightly different from the recognized results. Secondly, drive error
parameter identificationisaffected whenthedetectionchannelerroris
not fully compensated. Thirdly, insufficiently low circuit noise also af-
fects identification accuracy.
Drive error drift is mainly caused by external temperature changes.
Atthesametemperature,theerrorisstable.Inordertoensurethehigh
precision performance of the HRG in different environments, the drive
errorcanbeidentifiedunderdifferenttemperature gradientsaccording
tothemethodgiveninthispaper.Theerrorparametersattemperatures
betweenexperimentalsetpointsareobtainedbylinearinterpolation.In
actualoperation,thecurrentambienttemperaturecanbeobtainedfrom
the resonant frequency or by integrating a temperature sensor into the
control circuit. Dynamic compensation of the drive error according to
the ambient temperature avoids the situation where one-time adjust-
ments cannot counteract parameter drift.
Table 2 shows the identification results of drive error parameters at
different temperatures and the comparison between interpolation and
identification. The identification experiment in Table 1 is conducted
under 22℃ condition. To verify the above dynamic compensation
method,22℃errorparametersareobtainedthroughinterpolation.The
relativeerrorofinterpolationparametersisabout1%,whichmeetsthe
compensation requirements for temperature changes.
Fig.11andFig.12showthetestresultsofAllandeviationandscale
factor nonlinearity, respectively. After compensation with identifica-
tion, the bias instability decreases by 4 times from 0.0277◦/h to
0.0071◦/h, and the scale factor nonlinearity decreases by 5 times from
3.92ppm to 0.72ppm. The performance of interpolation parameters is
slightly worse than that of identification parameters, indicating the
implementation of dynamic compensation. In the initial experiment
before compensation, the initial azimuth is randomly selected. For the
subsequent compensated experiments, to ensure consistency, the same
azimuthasthatintheexperimentbeforecompensationischosen.Since
the circumferential drift of the standing wave caused by damping
anisotropy has been compensated, the overall performance of the gy-
roscope is independent of the azimuth.
We will continue to improve performance in the following areas.
Firstly, the drive error parameters may be affected by temperature.
Modeling and compensation will be carried out under variable tem-
perature conditions. Secondly, the damping and stiffness of the reso-
nator vary with ambient temperature. Improving the stability of the
above parameters will reduce HRG drift over time. Thirdly, the
manufacturing process is optimized to produce resonators with high-
quality factor and low frequency split. Small amplitude and quadra-
turecontrolforceislessdetrimentaltoperformancewhensystemerrors
are present.
7. Conclusion
Anaccurateerrormodelisestablishedbycomprehensivelyanalyzing
thecausesofdriveerrorinthewholeanglemodeHRG.Acompletesetof
errorparameteridentificationandcompensationmethodisgiven,which
can eliminatethedamageofthedriveerrorontheHRGbiasinstability
and scale factor nonlinearity. This method requires neither a specially
designed control scheme for parameter identification nor multiple iter-
ations to improve identification accuracy. It can be completed using a
conventionalHRGcontrolloop,significantlyimprovingtheefficiencyof
error compensation. Based on the above advantages, the proposed
method can be applied to the manufacture of large-scale HRG or other
Coriolis vibratory gyroscopes.
