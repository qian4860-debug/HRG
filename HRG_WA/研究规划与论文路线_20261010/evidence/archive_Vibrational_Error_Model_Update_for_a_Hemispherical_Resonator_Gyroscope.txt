Abstract—The problem statement for operation of a 
hemispherical resonator gyroscope under the impact of 
conventional vibration acceleration on the device housing in a 
non-axially symmetric formulation is considered. The necessity to 
take into account the non -axially symmetry of the gyroscope is 
shown, since it is the main source of the vibration error. The 
three-dimensional problem formulation is presented, which 
allows one to carry out  more sophisticated treatment of the 
effects caused by vibration in different planes. 
Keywords—hemispherical resonator gyroscope, vibration error, 
general error model. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The experimental research aimed at creating a 
hemispherical resonator gyroscope (HRG) began in the sixties 
of the last century;  but they are still of practical interest and 
continue to be relevant due to the great importance of HRG 
[1], [2]. A generally accepted HRG error model still does not 
exist [3]–[6], despite the fact that an attempt has already been 
made in [7] to develop an adequate error model. 
The main operation principle of the HRG consists in using 
the inertial properties of the standing wave, which is excited in 
an elastic axisymmetric shell (resonator). 
Considerable interest in technology to HRG is due to the 
fact that  it has a set of remarkable properties, namely: 
comparative simplicity of design, small dimensions, low 
power consumption, and potentially high accuracy [3]–[7]. 
In addition, HRG has a number of advantages traditionally 
noted in the specialized literature: 
 High accuracy of HRG, although somewhat less than 
that of conventional gyroscopes: with a rotating 
disk/rotor, fiber -optic gyroscopes, laser gyroscopes, 
and some other types of gyroscopes; 
 Sufficiently wide dynamic range of measurements; 
 Low sensitivity to linear overloads; 
 Resistance to mechanical shock and radiation; 
 Weak influence of temperature on the accuracy of the 
device; 
 Low power consumption; 
 High reliability, including the ability to work after 
short-term power outages; 
 Relatively short indication setting time. 
Nevertheless, the HRG has a number of disadvantages, 
namely: 
 Decrease in the resonator Q-factor due to metallization 
of the outer and inner surfaces; 
 Possibility of current leakage, leading to additional 
drift of the gyroscope; 
 The relatively small size of the working gaps between 
the surfaces of the upper base, the resonator, and the 
lower base significantly complicates the design 
features of the system necessary to maintain a high 
vacuum in the device; 
 High demands on the precision of manufacturing the 
resonator and the quality of the material, due to the 
sensitivity of the HRG to vibration. 
In modern literature, one can find publications on HRG, but 
their number is very small. In these publications, the following 
main areas of research can be distinguished. One group of authors 
studies the mechanism of excitation of wave effects in a resonator 
[8]–[10]. Other researchers consider methods used to extract 
information from the processes in the resonator itself [11], [12]. 
At the same time, there are practically no works devoted to the 
general model of HRG errors, apart from [7]. The authors of the 
presented work aim to build such an error model that would take 
into account the influence of various external inter ferences, in 
particular, vibrations. 
In the literature, as a rule, qualitative estimates of the HRG 
errors are given when exposed to vibration. To obtain qualitative 
estimates, most authors consider a two-dimensional (flat) model 
of a HRG resonator in the form of an elastic ring. At the same 
time, the issues of error model building for a considered 
gyroscope, which can take into account the influence of an 
external vibration source, remainson the agenda.

III. CONCLUSION 
The main result of this work is the developed mathematical 
model of the hemispherical resonator gyroscope, which can be 
used as a basis for verifying the compliance of a particular 
model of an HRG of this type with experimental data. In 
addition, the proposed model makes it possible to evaluate the 
influence of vibrations on the readings of a measuring device 
based on HRG at the stage of its design and development, 
using information on the vibrations level at the target object 
where this device will be operated. 
The paper considers the main ideas of a vibration error 
model development for HRG. The main result of the work is 
the obtained total error model (1) -(3) that takes into account 
the main source of uncertainties, namely, the vibration error. It 
should be noted that each component of the general error 
model requires additional theoretical and experimental studies.
