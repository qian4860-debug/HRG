Abstract—This study addresses the demand for the integration
and localization of control circuits for whole-angle mode
hemispherical resonator gyros, and carries out digital ASIC
(application specific integrated circuit) front-end design and
simulation verification work. Initially, focusing on the
requirement of whole-angle control, an investigation was
conducted into a whole-angle control scheme based on the
amplitude demodulation algorithm. Subsequently, to meet the
needs of frequency-phase control, a digital phase-locked loop
circuit based on the CORDIC algorithm was devised. Furthermore,
in response to the control requirements of energy and quadrature
signals, a study was carried out on a circuit based on the PI control
algorithm. Furthermore, to fulfill the requirement for detecting
the precession angle of the gyro resonator, an approach for
precession angle detection and whole-angle expansion method was
explored. Ultimately, the front-end design of the aforementioned
circuits and core modules functional verification were
accomplished on the IC (integrated circuit) design platform. The
validation results indicate that the designed circuits have
successfully fulfilled the intended functions and achieved favorable
performance metrics, thus laying a solid groundwork for the
subsequent back-end design and fabrication of digital ASICs.
Keywords-digital ASIC; hemispherical resonant gyro; whole-
angle mode; amplitude demodulation
I. INTRODUCTION
The hemispherical resonator gyro is a solid-state wave gyro
that operates based on the Coriolis effect, primarily used for
sensing angles and angular velocities. It is widely employed in
aerospace, navigation, weapon guidance, and various
specialized fields due to its advantages of high reliability,
precision, and long lifespan[1].
The whole-angle mode hemispherical resonator gyro refers
to the standing wave freely precessing in the resonator, directly
outputting angular information, which is equivalent to
integrating angular velocity [2]. Compared to traditional force
balance control modes, the whole-angle mode hemispherical
resonator gyro has a larger dynamic range and is suitable for
high-speed and high-dynamic applications [3], such as missile
guidance and aircraft navigation. However, the control scheme
for the whole-angle mode hemispherical resonator gyro is more
complex, making research on the control circuit of the whole-
angle mode hemispherical resonator gyro critically important.
Currently, the control circuits for whole-angle mode gyros
are primarily implemented using FPGA+CPU (ARM or DSP)
architectures[4][5],the first demodulation is done through FPGA
and the second demodulation and closed-loop control is done
through CPU. This circuit architecture exhibits robust
computational capabilities, yet it is concomitant with significant
power consumption, relatively low integration density, and
reliance on imported chips, consequently resulting in high circuit
costs.
Therefore, in order to achieve the integration and localization
of control circuit and reduce circuit costs, this study proposes a
digital ASIC as a new control circuit implementation scheme
that integrates demodulation and control in a single chip without
the aid of a CPU.

ANGLE MODE HEMISPHERICAL RESONANT GYRO
A. Wholel-angle mode hemispherical resonant gyro detection
principle
The schematic diagram of the two-piece hemispherical
resonator gyro structure is shown in Fig. 1. The cross-section of
the gyro resonator forms a flat capacitor with the flat electrode
below it. When an external angular velocity is applied, the gyro
resonator starts to precess freely, causing a change in the
capacitance of the flat capacitor. At this point, the amplified
circuit converts the slight change in capacitance into a voltage
variation, thereby extracting the detection signal from the gyro.

III. ASIC DESIGN FOR HEMISPHERICAL RESONANT GYRO
WHOLE-ANGLE CONTROL
The digital ASIC system, as illustrated in Fig. 3, includes the
following modules: ADC interface modules; a demodulation
module for extracting the primary standing wave energy
information, quadrature wave amplitude information, phase
difference information between the resonator's standing wave
signal and the carrier signal, and precession angle information of
the resonator; a frequency-phase control module that outputs a
carrier signal in phase with the resonator's standing wave signal;
an energy control module to stabilize the vibrational energy of
the resonator at a fixed value; an quadrature control module to
suppress the quadrature wave amplitude to zero; an angle
detection module for calculating the precession angle of the
resonator and outputting the angular components for quadrature
decomposition; a UART interface module for transmitting the
precession angle detected by the angle detection module to the
host computer and receiving control parameters; a signal
modulation, quadrature decomposition, and synthesis module
for modulating, decomposing, and synthesizing the gyro drive
signal; and  DAC interface modules.
This section focuses on the ASIC design of the four core
modules: frequency-phase control, energy control, quadrature
control and angle detection.

D. Angle detection
Figure 7.  Angle detection principle
The principle of angle detection, as shown in Fig. 7, is
primarily focused on calculating the precession angle 2q(n) of
the resonator and outputting 2q_q(n) for quadrature
decomposition. The precession angle 2q(n) needs to undergo
whole-angle expansion and 2q_q(n) is transformed from 2q(n)
to restrict the output range of 2q_q(n) to between 0° and 360°.
The specific process of calculating the precession angle is
outlined in Eq. (17), where 2q(n) represents the precession angle
result of the arctangent operation at the n-th clock cycle. The
arctangent calculation is realized using the CORDIC algorithm,
similar to the algorithm used in the NCO but with a distinction
in that this module takes sine and cosine values as input and
outputs angle values.

C. Quadrature control functional verification
The main process of quadrature control functional
verification is as follows: setting the output control signal qc(n)
of the quadrature control module to pass through a second-order
gyro system and feedback to the input of the energy control
module. This setup simulates the closed-loop operation of the
gyro's quadrature control. The clock frequency is set at 500kHz,
and the quadrature control reference value is set to 0. The control
process of the quadrature signal Q is illustrated in Fig. 11.
In Fig. 11, after 136ms, the quadrature signal Q is suppressed
to the reference value 0.

D. Angle detection functional verification
The main process of angle detection functional verification
involves changing the values of R(n) and S(n) every clock cycle
to simulate the rotation of the gyro resonator. When a forward
rotation speed of 22.5°/T (where T is the clock period) is inputted,
with the clock frequency set at 500kHz, outputs 2q(n) and
2q_q(n) are obtained as shown in Fig. 12.

Similarly, when a reverse rotation speed of 22.5°/T (where T

detection module successfully accomplishes the detection of the
precession angle and the whole-angle expansion of 2q(n) as well
as the restriction of 2q_q(n) to [0°, 360°], which is in line with
the expected expectations.

This study addresses the demand for integration and
localization of the whole-angle mode hemisphere resonant gyro
control circuit. It proposes a digital ASIC front-end design
scheme, and the results of core module functional verification
demonstrate:

frequency resolution of 0.0001164 Hz, achieves phase
tracking of the input signal within 100ms, with a phase
difference of 0.00028° at the 100ms.
