Abstract— Prolonging the pioneering works initiated in the 
60’s [1, 2] and bringing radical innovations such as planar 
electrodes, Safran Electronics & Defense has successfully 
achieved a major technological breakthrough by developing 
and industrializing a navigation-grade Inertial Navigation 
System (INS) technology based on HRG Crystal TM. Further 
optimization of the Cost Size Weight And Power (CSWAP) 
implies the development of a navigation grade MEMS 
gyroscope. Safran decided to take up the challenge. In this 
paper, we introduce a design evolution which has brought 
great performance results. While keeping an axisymmetric 
design and a fully balanced gyro mode [3], the innovation relies 
on electrostatic transducers now located between the vibrating 
masses. In the first part of this paper, we describe the key 
design rules and the innovation brought on electrostatic 
transducers location. The second part deals with the 
technological implementation of this new design on a silicon 
MEMS die. Finally, test results are presented with a special 
focus on performances under external vibrations. 
Keywords—MEMS, gyroscope, performance, navigation 
I. GYRO DESCRIPTION 
A. Design rules 
Safran MEMS Coriolis Vibrating Gyroscope (CVG) 
architecture relies on physical principles already applied on 
macroscopic sensors [4], which have notably contributed to 
the great success of HRG CrystalTM [5]. Namely: 
• An axisymmetric design of the resonator giving 
rise to degenerated vibrating modes minimizing 
frequency and damping anisotropies and enabling 
whole-angle mode of control. 
• Fully balanced gyro mode, resulting in zero 
parasitic force and torque on substrate at gyro 
frequency. This point is fundamental in order to 
reduce sensitivity to external vibrations and 
dissipation of energy in the substrate or with 
coupling with spurious modes.  
• High quality factor modes (long time constant of 
the resonator) to minimize the electrostatic energy 
required by the resonator and ensure low 
fundamental noise limit of the resonator 
(thermomechanical noise). 
B. Safran MEMS gyro architecture 
Safran MEMS gyro uses an axisymmetric design based 
on two equal concentric masses (m1, m2) linked together by  
 
Fig. 1. Safran MEMS CVG mechanical design. At gyro mode, m 1 and m 2 
vibrate in phase opposition 
2D-springs of stiffness k 0 and connected to a fixed frame by 
2D-springs of equal stiffness (k 1, k2).  This design gives rise 
to two in-plane translational modes of vibration, an 
unbalanced in-phase mode (frequency f 1) and a fully 
balanced gyro mode (frequency f 0) where m 1 and m 2 vibrate 
in antiphase. With the axisymmetric design, both modes ω 0 
and ω 1 are degenerated i.e. have two components with same 
frequencies and same mode shapes but in orthogonal 
directions. Gyro mode is fully balanced, as the center of 
gravity (COG) of the resonator remains fixed during 
vibration (Fig. 1). The frequency split between in-phase and 
anti-phase gyro modes depends on the ratio k 0/k1,2, which 
defines the degree of mechanical coupling between masses. 
Typical gyro mode is set above 10 kHz while in-phase mode 
is below 5 kHz. Thus, Safran MEMS gyro is set with a high 
coupling ratio to enhance isolation of gyro mode. That means 
that k1 and k 2 act as a suspension stage in order to limit the 
amplitude of external forces applied at the gyro mode 
frequency under external mechanical vibrations. External 
white noise vibrations applied in the vicinity of the gyro 
mode frequency are filtered and Angular Random Walk 
(ARW) performance is improved. Moreover, a high coupling 
ratio limits stress transmitted to the resonator under thermal 
environment, which is a source of transducer gain 
instabilities. Electrostatic transducers also follow 
axisymmetric implementation in order to nullify resultant 
quasi-static electrostatic forces on substrate. Electrostatic 
transducers are capacitive combs that allow the command 
and detection of the gyro mode vibration and the 
compensation of stiffness anisotropies due to imperfection of 
fabrication.

C.  The innovation: electrostatic transducers « between 
masses » 
 Usual detection of the gyro mode in CVG is made by  
differential sensing between electrostatic transduc ers 
measuring displacement of each mass relative to the  fixed 
frame. These transducers are composed of a fixed el ectrode 
mounted on the fixed frame and an electrode mounted on the 
vibrating masses (Fig.2 and Fig.3, left scheme). Ea sy to 
implement, this type of detection suffers from comm on 
modes errors under mechanical vibration, even for l ow gain 
anisotropies, due to partial rejection of non-linea r 
electrostatic terms excited by in-phase mode. An ea sy way 
would be to use higher frequency for the in-phase m ode, but 
this would imply a severe degradation of the decoup ling and 
filtering of the gyro mode. 
 To break this trade-off, Safran has developed a un ique 
transducer implementation greatly improving MEMS gy ro 
sensitivity to in-phase mode modulations. Electrost atic 
transducers have now been placed ‘between vibrating  
masses’ so that they directly detect the relative d isplacement 
between masses (amplitude and angle of the gyro mod e) 
giving an edge under harsh mechanical environments.  
Common-mode errors due to in-phase modes are elimin ated 
and noise performance is only limited by residual i mbalance 
of the gyro mode due to fabrication imperfections. As a 
result, the in-phase mode frequency can be kept low  for 
optimum effectiveness as a filtering and decoupling  stage, 
ensuring low bias and angular random walk with a gr eat 
robustness to external vibrations.  
II.  TECHNOLOGICAL IMPLEMENTATION  
A.  MEMS DIE 
Typical MEMS gyro dies are fabricated starting from  a 
SOI wafer. The single device layer is etched by Dee p 
Reactive Ion Etching (DRIE) to define the suspended  
masses and the transducers. The suspended masses an d the 
transducers are then released by etching the buried  oxide 
layer. Since both masses are electrically connected  by 
coupling springs and since detection requires at le ast two 
electric potentials, this implementation enables on ly the 
detection of the gap variation between an electrode  attached 
to the fixed frame and an electrode attached to the  mobile 
masses (Fig.2 and Fig. 3, left scheme). To enable a  direct 
capacitive detection between the suspended masses, at least 
two different electrical domains have to be defined  on each 
mass. The relative motion is then detected by the g ap 
variation between two electrodes located on each ma ss. To 
implement the new architecture, several technologic al 
solutions have been investigated. As required, the suspended 
masses have to be defined by a unique mechanical do main 
including or supporting multiple electrical domains . The 
adopted solution uses a two device layer configurat ion (Fig. 
3, right scheme). The bottom device layer provides the 
mechanical domain supporting multiple electrical do mains 
in the top device layer. The intermediate oxide lay er 
electrically isolates bottom and top device layers.  Trenches 
etched by DRIE isolate electrical domains in the to p device 
layer. The whole process leading to the MEMS resona tor 
can be summed up as follows. The mechanical device layer 
is etched before direct bonding of the top device l ayer. 
Then, the top device layer is etched to define the electrical 
domains. The release of the 2-layers suspended mass es is 
the key step of this process. A 2-step release is u sed in order 
to fully release the suspended mechanical domain fr om the 
handle wafer without releasing the electrical domai ns from 
the mechanical one. 
B.  MEMS assembly and packaging  
The MEMS die is attached and wired to a ceramic 
package using standard die attach and wire-bonding 
techniques compatible with the vacuum package. A si licon 
interposer is used between the MEMS die and the pac kage 
in order to reduce the impact of Coefficient of The rmal 
Expansion (CTE) mismatch between silicon and cerami c on 
the MEMS die. The ceramic package is a 44-Leaded Ch ip 
Carrier. U-shaped leads have been chosen to ensure both 
decoupling and alignment stability of the package w hen 
assembled on a printed circuit board. The MEMS reso nator 
has to operate at high and stable vacuum level (< 1 0 -3 mbar) 
to reduce gas damping. This is achieved at the pack age level 
using a custom vacuum sealing process of ceramic pa ckage 
and lid enclosing the MEMS die. The lid has a PaGeL id 
getter deposited on its inner surface. Prior to the  vacuum 
sealing, all the assembled parts have been baked at  a 
temperature below the getter activation temperature  to 
drastically reduce any outgassing sources. Inside a  chamber 
at ultra-low vacuum (<10 -5 mbar), the getter is then 
activated at high temperature just before the eutec tic lid 
sealing with the package.

III.  CHARACTERIZATION RESULTS  
A.  Mode of control 
Safran MEMS gyro uses electronics derived from well -
proven HRG Crystal TM  electronics enabling both whole-
angle (WA) and Force-To-Rebalance (FTR) modes. WA 
mode is promising for high performance as it provid es 
several advantages:  
• Low amplitude control signals are required which 
minimizes the impact of transducer gain variations 
on sensor performances 
• Low scale factor errors, only dependent on the 
instabilities of the gyroscopic coupling coefficien t 
of the two vibrating modes 
• High dynamic range, with no electronic limitation  
B.  Allan Variance 
Fig. 4 shows typical Allan deviation for a gyro at room 
temperature in static conditions. The signal was re corded for 
17 hours after warm-up. Gyro ARW is approximately 0 .004 
°/√h close to the expected thermomechanical noise. Bias 
instability is approximately 0.007 °/h at integrati on times of 
about 1000 s. This ARW complies with navigation-gra de 
application requirements. 
C.  Angular Random Walk (ARW) under random vibrations 
Fig. 5 shows the excellent stability of ARW under 
random vibrations. For the corresponding tests, a c onstant 
power spectral density (PSD) is applied from 10 Hz to 2 
kHz. Gyros have been vibrated in and out of the pla ne of the 
dies. The vibration levels are increased up to 14 g  RMS, 
corresponding to a PSD of 0.1 g²/Hz. Gyros are moun ted on 
a suspended IMU. The IMU suspensions have a resonan ce 
frequency of 500 Hz and a Q-factor of 10.

Under such high vibration levels, the ARW does not 
exceed 50 m°/√h. These levels are outstanding for M EMS 
and meet the requirements of high-end tactical or m id-end 
navigation applications. Further performance improv ements 
under vibrations are expected with slight chip desi gn tuning. 
An advantage of a fully balanced gyroscope is the r educed 
sensitivity of the ARW to vibration levels applied at the 
gyro mode frequency f 0. In Safran MEMS gyro, this 
characteristic is enhanced by the additional filter ing brought 
by the low-frequency in-phase mode that acts as a 
suspension stage. Therefore, ARW sensitivity to vib ration 
levels applied at gyro frequency remains below 50 m °/√h 
under harsh environments, typically a PSD of 10 -3 g²/Hz at 
10 kHz. 
D.  Thermal bias repeatability 
Bias has been measured over the -40 °C to 90 °C 
temperature range under a temperature ramp rate of 
0.5 °C/min. The data is binned in time intervals of  300 s at 
which noise is drastically reduced. A first thermal  cycle is 
used to compute a compensation model. This model is  then 
applied to correct the raw data obtained during the following 
cycles to obtain the compensated bias. Fig. 6 shows  the 
compensated bias of three gyros on a thermal cycle 
performed immediately after the calibration cycle. The 
following figure shows a compensated bias better th an 
0.1 °/h RMS overall temperature range for all three gyros.

E.  Bias rectification 
Bias rectification under random vibrations has been  
evaluated at room temperature. To perform these 
measurements, gyros are mounted on a suspended IMU (500 
Hz, Q=10) and have been vibrated in all directions.  A 
constant power spectral density (PSD) from 10 Hz to  2 kHz 
is applied up to 0.1 g²/Hz. The gyro bias has been 
compensated at room temperature. In Fig. 7, bias 
rectification is the slope of the integrated rate d uring shaker 
“ON” phase. It remains below 0.1 °/h up to 14 g RMS  for 
in-plane vibrations and below 1.1 °/h under 14 g RM S for 
out of plane vibrations. In the last OFF phase, we notice a 
slight slope on the integrated rate. It can be expl ained by a 
slight change in set up temperature. We also can no tice that 
the noise remains low even during ON phase, which i s 
coherent with our ARW measurement.

IV.  CONCLUSION  
The performances shown above are ranking Safran gyr o 
as one of the leading high performance MEMS gyros. The 
location of electrostatic transducers between the v ibrating 
masses has led to great performance under vibration s. Noise 
and bias remain low under random vibrations. Bias e rrors 
during temperature cycles are under 0.1 °/h RMS. Th ese 
levels of performance are already suitable for appl ications 
such as gyrocompassing AHRS, high-end tactical guid ance 
or high-end hybrid navigation applications even in harsh 
environments. Taking into account that the suitable  MEMS 
accelerometer [6] is already available, this gyro d esign 
opens the gate to navigation related applications w ith a 
disruptive CSWAP soon.
