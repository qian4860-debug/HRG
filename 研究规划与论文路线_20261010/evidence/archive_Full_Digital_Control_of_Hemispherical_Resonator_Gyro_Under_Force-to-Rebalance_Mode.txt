Full Digital Control of Hemispherical Resonator
Gyro Under Force-to-Rebalance Mode
Baomei Qiu, Member, IEEE, Jianwen Wang, Member, IEEE, and Penghua Li, Member, IEEE
Abstract— The signal processing of the hemispherical
resonator gyro (HRG) plays a key role in realizing the function
and improving the performance of HRG. In this paper, a novel
signal processing method for HRG is proposed and the basic prin-
ciple of this method is introduced. The hardware and software
design for the system are implemented based on the given method.
The system realizes the full digital circuit control strategy for
HRG under force-to-rebalance mode based on the digital signal
processor and ﬁeld programmable gate array framework. The
effectiveness of the proposed control strategy is veriﬁed by
simulations and experimental results. The experimental results
show that the gyro bias stability is better than 0.02°/h.
Index Terms — Hemispherical resonator gyro, digital control,
signal processing, force-to-rebalance control.
I. I NTRODUCTION
T
HE hemispherical resonator gyro (HRG) is used to
measure the angular rotation rate by the procession of
the vibration pattern based on the resonators Coriolis effect.
It utilizes the rotation sensitivity of the lowest bending mode
of a hemispherical shell. When HRG is vibrating in that
mode it will respond in one circle of the shell vibration.
The vibrating equatorial ellipse thus forms a standing wave
with four equally-spaced antinodes and four nodes. When
such a standing wave is present in the shell, a rotation about
the shell axis produces Coriolis forces on the vibrating mass
elements [1].
The HRG operates in one of two modes: whole angle (W A)
in which the gyro acts as an integrating rate gyro; FR in which
the gyro acts as a rate sensor. The choice of gyro operation is
highly dependent upon the application in which it will be used.
W A operation with its extremely accurate angular gain factor
will have excellent scale factor performance (< 1ppm) but will
have high angle noise due to lo w resolution and inaccuracies
in reading out the position of the standing wave. It also will
have bias errors that vary as the standing wave moves relative
to the case and requires compensation. On the other hand, FR
operation will have excellent noise characteristics and easily

The HRG has the features of small size, low noise, light
weight, high accuracy, long life span, inherent high reliability,
natural radiation hardness and no wear-out. With its excellent
performance, HRG has received i ncreasing attention and it

exploration and planetary exploration [3], [4]. HRG control
is required to establish a ﬁxed-amplitude standing wave, keep
the standing wave at a null position, remove any traveling
wave components in the wave, and synchronize itself with
the frequency of the resonator. Many researchers are devoted
to research related to it. Lynch has done much work in this
ﬁeld [1]. The detection of signals is one of the key techniques
for realizing the functions and characteristics of HRG. The

The standing wave parameters deriving from HRG are demod-
ulated by the reference signals VRS and VRC . The reference
signals are obtained from the reference voltage-controlled
oscillator. The frequency ω of the reference generator is
close to the natural frequency of HRG. This requires a
reference-phase loop to lock an external reference frequency
to the natural frequency of HRG [10], [11]. But the natural
frequency of HRG varies with the temperature. As men-
tioned in [12]–[14], the change rate of the natural resonance
frequency of HRG with the ambient temperature is about
80ppm/°C. As the environment temperature changes, it is
very difﬁcult to produce the frequency which is the same
as the natural resonance frequency ω of HRG. In addition,
the methods for demodulation need the high-accuracy
phase shifter. Therefore, it is difﬁcult to achieve the
conventional signals demodulation and control mode in
practice.
In this paper, the FR mode is used in order to meet the
needs of low noise and high res olution angular rate in satellite
applications. A novel demodulation method is presented based
on the FR mode. The the proposed approach, unlike the
conventional signal demodulation approaches, doesn’t require
the reference signals from the high accuracy phase-locked
loop (PLL) and the complex high accuracy phase shifting
circuit. The phase shifting circuit is mainly implemented based
on the digital signal processor (DSP) and the ﬁeld program-
mable gate array (FPGA). Therefore, the computational com-

II. R EALIZATION PRINCIPLE
A. Expression of HRG Signals
The HRG is a kind of Coriolis Vibratory Gyro (CVG) [14].
Its harmonic oscillator is a thin-walled axis-symmetric hemi-
spherical shell. Actually, it is a two-dimensional oscillator
with the natural frequency ω. In [15], the vibration model of
a vibratory gyroscope has been built. In [16], by applying the
ﬁnite element method, the equations of vibration have been
derived and analyzed. The design and analysis of a vibratory
gyroscope begin with the equations of motion for the excita-
tion and detection mode. The vibratory mode of HRG is based
upon the pendulum operation. As shown in Fig. 1, x and y
axes are the 0° electrode axis and the 45° electrode axis,
respectively. The proof-mass tra jectory is an orbital trajectory
m o t i o ni nt h ex-y plane with parameters θ, ϕ, a,a n d q,
describing the inclination angle, the orbital phase, semi-major,
and semi-minor axes, respectively. The parameters a and q can
be thought of as the amplitude of the main antinode and the
quadrature antinode of the proof-mass motion in x-y plane.
The parameter ϕ is the phase of main-wave. The parameter
ω is the angular frequency of the vibration of the HRG. The
signals x and y are measured through 0° and 45° electrode,
respectively. The controlled objects are two output signals
x and y from HRG: the amplitude of antinode point x is equal
to the signal a, the node signal y is asymptotically approaching
zero. The higher the control precision, the closer the signal y
is to zero (theoretically equal to zero). Under the FR mode,
the vibration displacement in the x and the y directions can
be expressed as follows [13]:

demodulation, the signal y plays an essential role. But the
signal y is a very weak signal. The higher the control precision,
the closer the signal y is to zero. The hardware implementation

To extract high precision information from the weak signals,
the digital acquisition circuit employed high-speed, high-
resolution analog-to-digital (A/D) converters. The wave node
signal y from the gauge outﬁt of HRG was highly ampli-
ﬁed before inputting into A/D converters, which signiﬁcantly
reduced the high-resolution demand for the A/D converter. The
buffer ampliﬁer employed the hig h precision instrumentation
ampliﬁer. In order to ensure the synchronization for the
discretization of the antinode and the node signals from the
gauge outﬁt, two pieces of high- speed and high-resolution
successive approximation analog-to-digital converters (SAR
ADC) AD7634 were employed. The sampling frequency was
500 kHz.
The digital processing circuit mainly used a Xilinx Virtex-4
ﬁeld-programmable gate array (FPGA) from Xilinx, Inc. The
functions of FPGA were mainly responsible for control-
ling A/D sampling, the ﬁnite-impulse-response (FIR) band-
pass ﬁlter after A/D sampling, the digital demodulation, the
digital modulation, the digital-to-analog (D/A) control, the
frequency detection of HRG, and data exchange with DSP,
etc. A TMS320C6701 DSP manufactured by TI Corporation
was employed. The functions of DSP were mainly responsible
for solving the signals a, q,a n dθ according to (6), completing
the proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control algorithm of
the amplitude loop, the quadrature loop, and the force control
loop for HRG control, IIR ﬁlter algorithm after FPGA demod-
ulation, temperature compensation, and linearity compensation
algorithms, etc. The results, as the output of HRG, were sent
to FPGA through the external bus.

sampling the antinode and the node signals. The signal x90,
as the key signal of demodulation mentioned above, is the
signal x delayed 90°. In practice, the zero-point drift for
HRG output varies with temperature. Therefore, it is of vital
importance to make necessary temperature compensation. The
frequency of HRG varies with temperature and each point
of temperature only corresponds to one natural frequency.
Therefore, instead of the temperature sensor, the temperature
drift compensation can be accomplished based on the natural
frequency of the resonator [12]. This compensation method
was valid and suitable for the full digital circuit control. The
resonant frequency of HRG changes slowly with temperature.
The frequency of HRG can be acquired by the multi-period
detection method. This method took the average values of
the detection results after periodically detecting HRG output
signals. It was very easy to implement with FPGA and a high-
resolution of 0.001 Hz was achieved. In practice, PLL achieved
the detection accuracy of 0.05° when the operating temperature
range for HRG is from −30 °C to 60 °C. This provided a solid
foundation for the high-precision control for HRG.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, a novel signal demodulation approach for
HRG was proposed. This demodulation approach doesn’t
require the reference signals from the high precision PLL.
The computational complexity was greatly reduced. The hard-
ware and software designs were implemented based upon
this method. The simulation and test results have proved the
feasibility of the approach and the correctness of its theoretical
derivation. The research results build a theoretical basis for
the HRG control and application under the FR mode. The full
digital control of HRG under the FR mode has been achieved
by the use of the FPGA and DSP digital demodulation
control mode. The experimental results show that the gyro bias
stability is better than 0.02 ◦/h. The experiment also found that
the three gyro control signals are coupled to each other under
the FR mode. The signal decoupling research will be further
needed to improve the gyro accuracy.
