Abstract: This study addresses the challenges of low efficiency and limited accuracy in the
frequency split identification of the high-precision hemispherical resonator gyro(HRG). A novel
high-precision and rapid identification method based on virtual precession is proposed. Initially,
the underlying causes of frequency split and its impact mechanism on the standing wave drift
of the HRG are systematically analyzed. Subsequently, a measurement approach leveraging
the active precession of the driven standing wave to determine the frequency split of the
HRG is introduced. The validity of the proposed method is further substantiated through
numerical simulations. Finally, an experimental system for HRG frequency split identification
is established, followed by identification experiments. Based on the experimental outcomes,
ion beam machining is employed to balance the resonator. Experimental results show a
significant reduction in frequency split after balancing, thereby confirming the effectiveness
and applicability of the proposed method.
Keywords: Hemispherical resonator gyro (HRG), Frequency split, Parameter estimation, Ion
beam balancing, vibration
1. INTRODUCTION
The hemispherical resonator gyro (HRG), as a high-
precision inertial sensor, has significant applications in
aerospace navigation, precision guidance, and attitude
control due to its long lifespan, low power consumption,
and excellent shock resistance Delhaye (2018), Delhaye
(2019), Rozelle (2009). The dynamic characteristics of its
core sensing element, the hemispherical resonator, directly
determine the performance of the gyro Jeanroy et al.
(2014). Among these characteristics, frequency split, a pa-
rameter representing the inherent defects of the resonator,
is a critical factor causing standing wave drift and limiting
the accuracy and stability of the gyro Lynch (1995).
In the field of hemispherical resonator frequency split
research, numerous scholars have conducted in-depth in-
vestigations. Huo et al. (2020),Wei et al. (2019) established
a dynamic model of the hemispherical resonator based
on the elastic thin-shell theory and derived the motion
equations considering frequency split. Their study revealed
that the fourth-order harmonic component of the mass
defect is the primary cause of frequency split, providing a
theoretical foundation for subsequent identification meth-
ods. Building on this, Wei et al. (2021), Li et al. (2020) pro-
posed a high-precision synchronous measurement method
based on the envelope of time-domain vibration signals,
which enables accurate identification of the hemispherical
resonator’s frequency split. However, this method cannot
directly determine the orientation of the resonator’s natu-
ral modal axes and requires additional identification using
Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), making the testing process
relatively complex. Furthermore, the presence of damping
asymmetry in the hemispherical resonator may introduce
errors in the identification of the natural modal axes and
frequency split. To address this issue, Huo et al. (2022) re-
fined the testing method, further improving identification
accuracy.
Once the amplitude and orientation of the fourth-order
harmonic component of the mass defect are determined,
mass balancing can be performed using either physi-
cal or chemical methods. Wang et al. (2018), Basarab
et al. (2015) implemented mass balancing using chemical
reagents, reducing the hemispherical resonator’s frequency
split to 0.05 Hz. In contrast, Huo et al. (2022) employed
ion beam balancing, achieving a more precise correction

on the elastic thin-shell theory and derived the motion
equations considering frequency split. Their study revealed
that the fourth-order harmonic component of the mass
defect is the primary cause of frequency split, providing a
theoretical foundation for subsequent identification meth-
ods. Building on this, Wei et al. (2021), Li et al. (2020) pro-
posed a high-precision synchronous measurement method
based on the envelope of time-domain vibration signals,
which enables accurate identification of the hemispherical
resonator’s frequency split. However, this method cannot
directly determine the orientation of the resonator’s natu-
ral modal axes and requires additional identification using
Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), making the testing process
relatively complex. Furthermore, the presence of damping
asymmetry in the hemispherical resonator may introduce
errors in the identification of the natural modal axes and
frequency split. To address this issue, Huo et al. (2022) re-
fined the testing method, further improving identification
accuracy.
Once the amplitude and orientation of the fourth-order
harmonic component of the mass defect are determined,
mass balancing can be performed using either physi-
cal or chemical methods. Wang et al. (2018), Basarab
et al. (2015) implemented mass balancing using chemical
reagents, reducing the hemispherical resonator’s frequency
split to 0.05 Hz. In contrast, Huo et al. (2022) employed
ion beam balancing, achieving a more precise correction
that successfully reduced the frequency split from an initial
0.2354 Hz to 0.0006 Hz.
Although existing studies have attempted to reduce fre-
quency split through mechanical balancing and mass com-
pensation, traditional methods generally suffer from low
efficiency and insufficient identification accuracy, making
it difficult to meet the urgent demand for rapid calibration
and correction of resonator dynamics in high-precision
HRGs. The envelope-based frequency split testing method
for hemispherical resonators is closely related to the fre-
quency split itself. The smaller the frequency split, the
longer the testing duration, exhibiting an inverse relation-
ship between the two. For a hemispherical resonator with
a frequency split of 0.001 Hz, a single test requires more
than 1000 seconds.
To address these challenges, this study proposes a novel
high-precision and rapid identification method for HRG
frequency split based on virtual precession, aiming to over-
come the limitations of traditional techniques. First, by
systematically analyzing the causes of frequency split and
its impact mechanism on standing wave drift, the intrinsic
relationship between the active precession characteristics
of the driven standing wave and frequency split is revealed.
Building on this, an innovative rapid measurement model
for frequency split is constructed based on the dynamic
response of the resonator, and the robustness and feasi-
bility of the theoretical model are verified through numer-
ical simulations. Furthermore, an experimental platform
for HRG frequency split identification is established, and
dynamic balancing of the resonator is performed using ion
beam machining.

3. FREQUENCY SPLIT IDENTIFICATION METHOD
BASED ON VIRTUAL PRECESSION
As is shown in Fig. 2, the basic control scheme of the
HRG with a rate integration formulation includes phase-
locking, quadrature, and amplitude control loops Wang
et al. (2025), Sun et al. (2025), Wang et al. (2024).
These loops collectively ensure the stable standing wave
state of the resonator, enabling the measurement of the
input angular velocity. Based on this, a virtual precession
loop is added to apply control forces, actively controlling
the standing wave to achieve high-precision identification
of the hemispherical resonator’s frequency split and the
fourth-order harmonic of mass defects. The derivative of

5. CONCLUSION
This paper presents a virtual precession-based method-
ology to overcome the persistent challenges of inefficient
and low-precision frequency split identification in high-
precision HRG. A systematic investigation into the ori-
gin of frequency split and its correlation with standing
wave drift lays the theoretical foundation for the proposed
method. By innovatively harnessing the controlled active
precession of driven standing waves, the developed ap-
proach enables rapid and accurate frequency split quantifi-
cation, surpassing conventional identification paradigms.
Numerical simulations and experimental validation con-
firm its robustness, with post-identification ion beam ma-
chining achieving a substantial reduction in resonator fre-
quency split. The demonstrated efficacy and operational
adaptability of this method not only advance HRG perfor-
mance optimization but also provide a scalable framework
for precision enhancement in inertial navigation systems.
