Abstract—The performance of the hemispherical resonator
gyro (HRG) in whole-angle (W A) mode is a ﬀected by the angle-
dependent harmonic drift. Suppressing this drift is essential
to improve its temperature stability and operational range. In
this article, a novel real-time method for eliminating harmonic
drift based on a forward and reverse precession (FRP) control
scheme is proposed. First, the sources of harmonic drift in
HRG are analyzed. The HRG dynamical equations incorporating
multiple error sources are derived and analyzed through numer-
ical simulation. Subsequently, a real-time error identiﬁcation
algorithm is developed and validated through simulation. Finally,
a temperature experiment is conducted to validate the method.
The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method
can accurately identify and compensate for errors at di ﬀerent
temperatures, e ﬀectively suppressing the harmonic components
in angular velocity by more than 97%. The bias instability
(BI) of the HRG remains consistently around 0 .024◦/h across
various temperatures, indicating strong temperature stability.
Additionally, the scale-factor nonlinearity (SFN) is reduced ﬁve-
fold to 0.84 ppm. Most importantly, this method can be applied
to all Coriolis vibratory gyroscopes to e ﬀectively eliminate their
common angle-dependent harmonic drift.
Index Terms —Angle-dependent harmonic drift, forward and
reverse precession (FRP) control scheme, hemispherical resonator
gyro (HRG), real-time compensation.
I. I NTRODUCTION
T
HE hemispherical resonator gyro (HRG) is one of the
most promising inertial sensors for high-precision nav-
igation due to its high accuracy, high reliability, and long
service life [1], [2]. It has become the primary choice for many
space missions and industrial applications when high accuracy
and long-term stability are critical [3], [4]. The HRGs are
mainly classiﬁed into two categories according to the operating
principles [5]: force-to-rebalance (FTR) mode and whole-
angle (W A) mode. Diﬀerent from FTR mode, which ﬁxes the
standing wave at a speciﬁc position, the standing wave can
precess freely in W A mode. The W A mode has the advantages

mainstream [9]. However, the W A-HRG has strict symmetry
requirements, including the symmetry of damping/stiﬀness and
the consistency of the channels. This places higher demands on
the resonator processing, circuit design, as well as the control
and compensation algorithms [10].
One of the key factors limiting the performance of W A-HRG
is the angle-dependent harmonic drift [9], which signiﬁcantly
aﬀects the measurement accuracy and dynamic performance
of HRG. Previous studies have shown that the main causes of
the harmonic drift are as follows [7], [11], [12].
1) Electrode assembly error.
2) Capacitive sensing and actuation nonlinearity.
3) Damping and sti ﬀness asymmetry.
Many studies on electrode assembly error analyze the detec-
tion and driving chains in isolation and ignore their coupling
eﬀects [13], [14], [15]. In [11] and [16], the assembly error is
eﬀectively separated and identiﬁed through o ﬄine calibration.
These methods are adopted for comparison with the online
identiﬁcation results in this article. Vatanparvar and Shkel [17]
proposed a self-calibration method for gain mismatch based
on the amplitude control force in the two axes. However, the
method cannot accurately identify the gain mismatch in the
presence of assembly angle error, which causes signal coupling
between channels. Yan et al. [18] and Gu et al. [19] identiﬁed
drive-related errors through virtual precession. However, the
method requires prior calibration of the detection errors, and
the identiﬁcation accuracy under unidirectional precession
is a ﬀected by the structural imperfection of the resonator.
Chen et al. [20] proposed an online identiﬁcation and com-
pensation approach for drive and phase errors in W A-HRG
based on sinusoidal self-excitation modulation. However, the
sinusoidal disturbance reduces measurement accuracy and
introduces additional control errors, resulting in unnecessary
drift. Wang et al. [21] proposed a method for real-time
identiﬁcation of assembly error using virtual rotation modula-
tion through control voltage. However, their method performs
only oﬄine identiﬁcation without online compensation, which
limits its applicability. The method proposed in this article
enables online identiﬁcation and compensation of assembly
error by introducing the existing compensation values into the
identiﬁcation model. Furthermore, the limitations and tradeoﬀs

Many scholars believe that the temperature characteristic of
the capacitive nonlinearity is stable, so they concentrate on the
oﬄine calibration methods. Hu and Gallacher [12] proposed
a correction method for capacitive sensing nonlinearity in
MEMS rate-integrating gyroscopes, but actuation nonlinearity
remains unsolved. Wang et al. [22] analyzed the harmonic drift
induced by assembly error and capacitive sensing /actuation
nonlinearity, and proposed an oﬄine calibration method. How-
ever, the analysis treats the errors independently and neglects
their coupling eﬀects, resulting in residual errors in the calibra-
tion results. Gao et al. [23] analyzed the distortion mechanisms
in capacitive detection and proposed a calibration method
based on a recursive least squares (RLS) algorithm. However,
the experimental validation is limited to speciﬁc operating
conditions, and the model’s robustness under broader dynamic
environments remains insu ﬃciently explored. Yi et al. [24]
proposed a parameter identiﬁcation method based on chaotic
technology and particle swarm optimization algorithm to
suppress nonlinear harmonic drift. However, the proposed
method does not account for temperature variations, and no
temperature experiments have been conducted to evaluate its
robustness under varying temperature conditions. Sun et al.
[25] and Yu et al. [26] proposed a dynamic compensation
method for capacitive nonlinearity by demodulating the ampli-
tude of the third harmonic in the detection signal. However,
the study focuses on microshell resonator gyroscopes, and the
applicability of the method to larger scale HRGs with distinct
structural and dynamic characteristics remains to be veriﬁed.
This article proposes another approach to identify capacitive
nonlinearity, and its e ﬀectiveness has been experimentally
veriﬁed on HRGs.
In [7], [27], and [28], angular drift caused by sti ﬀness
and damping imperfection is addressed through the quadrature
nulling loop and velocity feedback control. However, these
methods are based on a linear model of gyroscopes and do
not take into account capacitive nonlinearity. Pi and Bang
[29] developed an observer to estimate damping and sti ﬀ-
ness imperfection parameters based on the dynamic model.
However, the simulation results reveal that the method is
highly model-dependent and is sensitive to the initial values.
Guo et al. [30] proposed an adaptive control algorithm to
recognize the damping asymmetry parameter through angu-
lar velocity. However, this approach only ensures accuracy
when the external angular velocity exhibits minimal variation.
Yan et al. [18] and Gu et al. [19] compensated for the drift
caused by damping asymmetry based on the output of the
amplitude control loop. However, the e ﬀectiveness of their
compensation method depends on the accuracy of prior error
calibration, which is not performed online. The algorithm
proposed in this article enables online identiﬁcation and com-
pensation of prior errors, which enhances the suppression of
the drift induced by sti ﬀness and damping asymmetry.
The experimental evidence demonstrates that electrode
assembly error, capacitive sensing /actuation nonlinearity, and
damping/stiﬀness asymmetry exhibit temperature-dependent
variations [10], [26], [31]. The variations cannot be ignored for
high-precision gyroscopes. According to the above summary,
most existing studies ignore the coupling between multiple
errors and lack real-time compensation, which limits the
performance of vibratory gyroscopes in di ﬀerent environ-
ments. To address these issues, a novel method based on
the forward and reverse precession (FRP) control scheme
is proposed in this article for the real-time elimination of
angle-dependent harmonic drift. It achieves harmonic drift
suppression by online identifying and compensating for the
relevant error terms. Compared with o ﬄine calibration meth-
ods, the proposed algorithm can track and compensate for
temperature-induced changes in the error terms. In contrast
to other identiﬁcation methods based on self-precession, this
article considers the issue of real-time compensation under
practical applications. It takes into account the e ﬀects of
existing compensation values and external angular velocity on
the identiﬁcation process, which have not been discussed in
previous studies. In addition, the multierror model established
in this article further improves the e ﬀectiveness of harmonic
drift suppression. The experimental results demonstrate that
the method e ﬀectively eliminates the harmonic drift under
varying temperatures, signiﬁcantly improving the temperature
stability of the HRG.

C. FRP Control Scheme
This article adopts a single-channel control strategy based
on time division multiplexing and shared detection /driving
electrodes [34] as shown in Fig. 1, including amplitude control
for energy maintaining, quadrature control for quadrature
nulling, and a phase-locked loop for resonance tracking. The
detection and driving signals of the X and Y channels are
sequentially applied to the same set of electrodes, while
passing through their respective shared analog circuits for
detection and driving.
This control strategy fundamentally eliminates the electrical
gain and phase imbalance between channels. As a result, the
gain mismatch and angle error originate solely from assembly
error, and each exhibits the same magnitude in both the
detection and driving chains.

Fig. 14 shows the Allan variance of HRG under the conven-

ratio of 37.50% and 93.75%, respectively. In contrast, the BI
of HRG under the FRP control scheme remains consistently
around 0.024◦/h, reﬂecting strong temperature stability. When
signiﬁcant harmonic components are present in the output,
the Allan variance curve becomes distorted and can no longer
represent the true random characteristics. Under the traditional
control scheme, the tested HRG exhibits a total standing wave
drift of about 8 ◦ during a 4-h test for Allan variance, which
is much smaller than one period of the fourth harmonic.
Therefore, the reasonable Allan variance curves can be plotted.
When the proposed method based on the FRP control scheme
is applied, more than 97% of the fourth and eighth harmonic
components are eliminated. The residual harmonic compo-
nents are compensated at the gyroscope output using a ﬁxed
compensation table. The reasonable Allan variance curves
depicted in Fig. 14(b) demonstrate that the remaining harmonic
components are negligible under stationary conditions.
Under dynamic conditions, as analyzed in Section III-C,
the harmonic amplitude increases linearly with the external
angular velocity. Therefore, the harmonic drift a ﬀects the
output accuracy and the scale-factor nonlinearity (SFN) of
HRG. As shown in Fig. 15, the real-time compensation method
reduces the SFN ﬁvefold at 20 ◦C, from 4.13 to 0.84 ppm.
V. C ONCLUSION
This article proposes a novel real-time method for eliminat-
ing angle-dependent harmonic drift based on an FRP control
scheme. First, it is determined that the harmonic drift is
mainly caused by electrode assembly error, capacitive sens-
ing/actuation nonlinearity, and damping /stiﬀness asymmetry.
The dynamical equations of HRG under multiple errors and
their compensation values are derived. The contribution of
each error to the harmonic components in angular velocity is
analyzed by numerical simulation. Subsequently, an algorithm
based on harmonic decomposition of the amplitude control
force for real-time identiﬁcation and compensation of the
relevant errors is proposed and analyzed through simulation.
Finally, the e ﬀectiveness of the novel method is veriﬁed
through temperature experiments. The harmonic drift is sup-
pressed by over 97% across di ﬀerent temperatures, and the
tested HRG achieves a stable BI of approximately 0 .024◦/h.
Moreover, the SFN of the HRG is reduced ﬁvefold, from
4.13 to 0.84 ppm. The proposed real-time harmonic drift
elimination method signiﬁcantly enhances the temperature
stability of the HRG and broadens its practical applications.
Most importantly, this method can be applied to all Coriolis
vibratory gyroscopes to e ﬀectively eliminate their common
angle-dependent harmonic drift.
This study presents a method based on the FRP control
scheme for real-time elimination of the harmonic drift, achiev-
ing promising results. Future work will focus on improving
the FRP control implementation, such as accurately setting
the virtual precession speed and reﬁning the transition process
between FRP processes, to further enhance the performance
of the HRG.
